The second approach proposed a mode of objective observation — the camera moving through the location as if conducting a survey, registering the mold's damage and the fractured surfaces it had produced without subordinating them to Jonas's psychology or narrative causality. Walls, frames, corners, and material boundaries were treated as sites of evidence rather than dramatic space. The decoupage proposed a kind of inventory logic: each shot a record of what the organism had done to a specific surface, assembled in sequence so the accumulation of damage could be read spatially across the location rather than emotionally through a character.
